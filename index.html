World News

Washington’s Russia-sanctions waiver is reshaping the start of the week. President Donald Trump’s October 16 executive order waives sanctions, restrictions and duties under the new Graham Act, citing the national interest. Reuters reports that Secretary of State Marco Rubio said officials would use up to 60 days to evaluate the economic impact. The waiver is a policy decision; a negotiated end to the war in Ukraine remains uncertain.

China reported third-quarter economic growth of 4.5% from a year earlier, up from 4.3% in the second quarter. Its National Bureau of Statistics puts growth for the first nine months at 4.6%. September industrial output rose 5.5% year over year, while Reuters reports retail-sales growth of just 1.1%. Those figures point to a stronger production side than consumer spending, despite the improvement in headline GDP.

Energy supply remains a separate risk. Reuters reports that Iran said a tanker caught fire and exploded after striking a mine in the southern Strait of Hormuz; that account is attributed to Iranian authorities. Its morning market report also describes record tanker rates and higher insurance costs. Expectations of more Russian supply may ease prices, but they do not resolve disruption to Gulf shipping.

Wall Street begins Monday after its toughest week for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq since June. Friday’s S&P decline was 1.1%, the Nasdaq lost 2.1%, and the Dow fell 0.3%, according to AP. Over the week the Nasdaq fell 4.5%, while the Dow gained 0.7%. The divergence shows that selling was particularly severe in technology rather than equally spread across major indexes.

Reuters’ Friday report describes concern that heavily indebted AI-infrastructure companies may not generate profits quickly enough to service their borrowing. Its Monday morning outlook puts Meta, Tesla, Netflix, Lam Research and Texas Instruments among this week’s earnings tests. Meta’s stated 2026 capital-spending range is $130 billion to $145 billion. These upcoming results will provide evidence about spending and demand; their outcomes are not yet known.

The broader economy offered a firmer data point Friday: the Federal Reserve’s preliminary September report showed industrial production rising 0.7% from August, including a 0.3% increase in manufacturing. Capacity utilization reached 76.9%, still 2.5 percentage points below its long-run average. That report measures real output, a different signal from the market’s reassessment of technology valuations.

Tech News

Nvidia said Friday that RTX Spark laptops are available from ASUS, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Microsoft and MSI. The company advertises up to 128 GB of unified memory and one petaflop of FP4 AI performance, aimed at running models locally alongside creative and gaming workloads. These are manufacturer specifications and performance claims, rather than independent benchmark results.

Microsoft’s October 12 infrastructure announcement sets a different course for its own cloud services: Maia 200 is serving production workloads, including Copilot, and the company plans multi-gigawatt Maia deployments starting in 2027. It expects Maia 300 to deliver 40% more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU-based systems. That efficiency comparison is Microsoft’s forecast; the announced deployment timetable is still ahead.

Google Research outlined an effort Friday to connect AI agents to laboratory robots through VERID, a system that tracks equipment geometry, liquid volumes and reagent inventories before commands reach the hardware. Its researchers describe closed-loop experiments in molecular design, with simulations and logs used to catch errors. The work is experimental: it illustrates progress toward agents that test hypotheses physically, without establishing that autonomous laboratories are broadly ready for deployment.

Sports

Miami takes over No. 1 in Sunday’s AP poll after beating Florida State 43–35 in double overtime. BYU rises to No. 2 following its 28–24 win over Notre Dame, with Ohio State third after beating Indiana 19–15. AP reports that five of the previous top seven lost, the first such week in the poll’s 90-year history. Miami received 40 first-place votes, BYU 20, Ohio State nine and Texas Tech one.

The decisive swing in Provo came with 1:47 remaining, when BYU finished a 10-play, 55-yard touchdown drive to erase Notre Dame’s 24–21 lead, according to the Irish’s game report. Notre Dame’s final end-zone pass fell incomplete. The result leaves BYU unbeaten at 6–0 and Notre Dame at 6–1; the Irish remain fourth in the new poll.

The shakeup extends well beyond the top three: Georgia falls to fifth after losing to Auburn, Alabama drops to 13th after its loss at Tennessee, and Texas slides to 19th after falling to Florida. The Gators return at No. 20, while Arizona enters at No. 25. These AP media rankings are separate from the College Football Playoff committee’s selections.
